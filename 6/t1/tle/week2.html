Government Agencies in Fisheries and Aquaculture

Understanding how the government supports fish raisers and promotes sustainable fishing in the Philippines.

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify at least three government agencies that support fisheries and aquaculture.
  2. Describe the specific forms of assistance each agency provides to fish raisers.
  3. Explain why government support is important for sustainable fishing practices.
Key Concept

Why Government Agencies Matter

In the Philippines, millions of Filipinos depend on fisheries and aquaculture for their livelihood and food security. Government agencies exist to make sure that fish raisers — from small backyard fish pond operators to large aquaculture businesses — have the support they need to succeed and to practice responsible stewardship of our aquatic resources.

Government agencies do not just regulate fishing — they actively help communities grow. From providing fingerlings (baby fish) for stocking, to training farmers on modern techniques, to protecting the environment from overfishing, these agencies play a vital role in building a thriving and sustainable fish-raising industry.

Core Agencies

Key Government Agencies and Their Roles

BFAR

Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources — provides technical training, fingerlings, fishing equipment, and enforces fisheries laws to promote sustainable practices.

DA

Department of Agriculture — oversees agriculture-related fishery programs and provides financial and technical assistance to farming communities.

DENR

Department of Environment and Natural Resources — protects aquatic ecosystems, enforces environmental laws, and manages coastal and marine areas.

LGU

Local Government Units — implement local fisheries ordinances, manage municipal waters, and deliver livelihood programs to local fish raisers.

Forms of Assistance

How the Government Helps Fish Raisers

Government support comes in many forms. Understanding these can help you, as a fish raiser or future fish raiser, take full advantage of what is available to you:

  • Technical Assistance: Experts visit fish farms to advise on better methods of fish care and disease prevention.
  • Training Programs: Seminars and workshops on modern fish farming techniques, water quality management, and post-harvest handling.
  • Loans and Financial Support: Low-interest loans through agencies like LandBank and DBP help fish raisers buy equipment and expand their operations.
  • Fingerlings and Feeds: Government hatcheries distribute fingerlings — baby fish — to stock fish ponds, often at low or no cost to qualified farmers.
  • Livelihood Programs: Community-based programs that help displaced or marginalized fisherfolk start or improve their fish-raising projects.
  • Research and Development: BFAR and state universities conduct research on new fish species, feeding techniques, and disease treatments, then share results with farmers.
  • Environmental Protection: Enforcement of laws against illegal fishing, coral reef protection, and mangrove reforestation to ensure a healthy environment for aquatic life.
  • Marketing Assistance: Trade fairs, market linkages, and promotion of fish products to help farmers sell their harvest at fair prices.

Non-Government Organizations (NGOs) in Fisheries

Discovering how private organizations partner with communities and government to protect and develop aquatic resources.

Learning Objectives

  1. Define what an NGO is and how it differs from a government agency.
  2. Identify the types of assistance NGOs provide to fishing communities.
  3. Analyze why cooperation between government, NGOs, and communities is essential for sustainable fisheries.
Key Concept

What Is an NGO?

A Non-Government Organization (NGO) is a private, non-profit group that works to help people and protect the environment — not to earn money for owners or shareholders. Unlike government agencies, NGOs are not run by the government. They are usually funded by donations, grants, and membership fees.

In the Philippines, many NGOs focus on helping fishing communities, protecting marine ecosystems, promoting sustainable livelihoods, and educating fisherfolk and fish raisers about better practices. Some well-known NGOs in the fisheries sector include Haribon Foundation, Coastal Conservation and Education Foundation (CCEF), and WWF-Philippines.

Forms of Support

How NGOs Help Fishing Communities

Education

NGOs conduct community workshops, produce learning materials, and train fisherfolk on sustainable fishing methods and environmental care.

Livelihood Support

They help communities develop alternative livelihoods such as seaweed farming, eco-tourism, and fish processing to reduce pressure on fish stocks.

Environmental Conservation

NGOs lead mangrove reforestation, coral reef rehabilitation, and marine protected area (MPA) management in partnership with communities.

Technical Assistance

They provide expert advice on water quality, fish health, eco-friendly fish raising methods, and sustainable aquaculture technologies.

Cooperation

Why Cooperation Is Key

No single organization — whether government or NGO — can solve all the challenges facing Philippine fisheries alone. Sustainable fisheries are built on cooperation among three key groups:

  • Government Agencies — provide laws, funding, and technical support
  • NGOs — bring grassroots engagement, environmental expertise, and community organizing
  • Community Members — implement practices, report illegal activities, and sustain local traditions

When all three work together, the result is stronger: fish stocks recover, communities prosper, and aquatic ecosystems are preserved for future generations.

✅ Answer saved for your review.

Personal and Entrepreneurial Characteristics of Successful Fish Raisers

Exploring the values, habits, and mindset that drive success in fish raising.

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify at least five personal and entrepreneurial characteristics of successful fish raisers.
  2. Evaluate how each characteristic contributes to overcoming challenges in fish raising.
  3. Reflect on which characteristics you already have and which you can develop further.
Character Matters

Why Character Drives Success

Fish raising is more than just filling a pond with fish and waiting to harvest them. It requires daily attention, wise decisions, the ability to handle setbacks, and the discipline to follow through on plans. Knowledge alone is not enough — a successful fish raiser must also possess strong personal and entrepreneurial characteristics.

These characteristics are not things people are simply born with. They are habits and values that can be learned, practiced, and developed over time — starting right now, in school and in the community.

Key Traits

Essential Characteristics

🌅 Diligence

Working hard and consistently every day — monitoring water quality, feeding fish on schedule, and maintaining equipment — even when results are slow.

⏳ Patience

Fish raising takes time. A successful fish raiser waits for fish to grow to market size without rushing the harvest and losing profit.

📋 Responsibility

Taking ownership of both successes and failures. A responsible fish raiser does not blame others when things go wrong but instead looks for solutions.

📚 Willingness to Learn

Attending seminars, reading extension bulletins, and asking experts for advice to continuously improve fish raising methods.

📊 Proper Planning

Setting production goals, scheduling feeding and harvesting, managing cash flow, and preparing for risks such as floods and disease outbreaks.

🌿 Environmental Stewardship

Caring for the water, soil, and surrounding ecosystem as an investment in long-term productivity, not just a regulation to follow.

💰 Financial Management

Keeping records of income and expenses, avoiding unnecessary debt, and reinvesting profits wisely to grow the fish-raising enterprise.

Success Formula

What Brings Lasting Success

Success in fish raising is not a lucky accident. It results from three things working together:

  1. Continuous Learning — Always updating knowledge about new techniques, market trends, and environmental changes.
  2. Responsible Practices — Using resources wisely, following laws, and treating workers and the environment with respect.
  3. Community and Organizational Support — Building relationships with neighbors, government agencies, and NGOs to get help and share knowledge.

Sustainable Fish Raising Through Cooperation and Problem-Solving

Putting it all together: how fish raisers, government, NGOs, and communities build a thriving, responsible industry.

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain what sustainable fish raising means and why it matters for future generations.
  2. Analyze how different stakeholders cooperate to solve problems in fish raising.
  3. Create a simple action plan to address a specific challenge in fish raising using available resources.
Big Picture

What Is Sustainable Fish Raising?

Sustainable fish raising means producing fish in a way that meets the needs of today without destroying the resources that future generations will need. It balances three things:

  • Economic Sustainability: Fish raising must be profitable so farmers can continue their livelihood.
  • Environmental Sustainability: Aquatic resources — water, fish, coral, mangroves — must be protected and replenished.
  • Social Sustainability: Benefits must reach communities fairly, and practices must respect local culture and traditions.

No single person, organization, or agency can achieve all three alone. That is why cooperation is the foundation of sustainable fish raising.

Stakeholders

Everyone Has a Role

Fish Raisers

Apply sustainable methods, keep records, report problems early, and participate actively in community organizations and cooperative programs.

Government Agencies

Enforce laws, fund research, distribute resources, and coordinate large-scale programs that no individual farmer or NGO can do alone.

NGOs

Bridge the gap between government policies and community realities, provide hands-on training, and advocate for sustainable practices at all levels.

Community

Monitor local resources, practice bantay-dagat, support cooperative buying and selling, and pass down environmental values to younger generations.

Problem-Solving

How Successful Fish Raisers Solve Problems

Problems in fish raising are unavoidable — floods, disease, falling fish prices, water pollution. What separates successful fish raisers from those who give up is how they respond. Here is a five-step approach used by the best fish raisers in the Philippines:

  1. Planning: Anticipate risks before they happen. Set contingency budgets and maintain backup supplies.
  2. Perseverance: Stay committed during hard times. View failures as lessons, not defeats.
  3. Continuous Learning: Seek new information from BFAR, NGOs, and fellow fish raisers after every challenge.
  4. Responsible Resource Management: Never overstock ponds, never use banned chemicals, and always maintain water quality standards.
  5. Collaboration: Reach out to neighbors, cooperatives, government technicians, and NGO workers. No fish raiser succeeds alone.

Learning Activity 1

Build a Simple Action Plan

Challenge
A group of fish raisers in your barangay has noticed that their tilapia is dying in large numbers every summer when water temperatures rise. They have no idea what to do, and they cannot afford to lose another harvest.
✅ Action plan saved!
Guide Answer:
Cause: High summer temperatures reduce dissolved oxygen in the water, causing fish to suffocate — a condition called thermal stratification.
Assistance: BFAR technicians can test water quality and recommend aeration systems; LGU may provide emergency fingerlings after the loss; an NGO like CCEF might offer training on heat-resilient farming methods.
Immediate actions: Install paddlewheels or air pumps to aerate the water; reduce stocking density; feed fish early morning and late afternoon when temperatures are cooler; shade part of the pond with nets.
Prevention: Monitor water temperature daily during summer; join a cooperative to share the cost of aeration equipment; plant trees around pond edges for natural shade; participate in BFAR climate-resilient aquaculture training.

Let's Learn the Parts

The mouth is where a fish takes in food. Its shape and position tell us what and how the fish eats.

The eyes give the fish good vision — fish don't have eyelids, so they never blink!

Behind the eye is the operculum, a hard flap that protects the gills and pumps water over them so the fish can breathe.

Fish have several kinds of fins:

  • Dorsal fin (top) — keeps the fish upright, like a balancing pole.
  • Pectoral fins (sides) — help the fish steer and stop.
  • Pelvic fins (belly) — add extra balance.
  • Caudal fin (tail) — the main "engine" that pushes the fish forward.

The lateral line runs along the fish's side and senses movement in the water — it helps the fish feel danger nearby, even in muddy water.

Scales cover the body for protection, and a slippery layer of mucus helps the fish glide and stay safe from germs.

Let's Learn the Organs

The gills are made of thin filaments filled with blood vessels. This is where oxygen from water enters the fish's blood, and carbon dioxide leaves — the same gills that the operculum protects from outside.

The heart is a simple two-chambered pump. It sends blood to the gills for oxygen, then out to the rest of the body.

The swim bladder is a gas-filled sac. It lets the fish float at a certain depth without always having to swim — this answers our Engage question!

Food travels through the stomach and intestine to be digested, with help from the liver. The kidney removes waste and helps balance water and salt in the fish's body.

The backbone (spinal cord) runs along the top of the fish, protecting important nerves and supporting its whole body shape.

Let's Learn About Fish Diseases

Fish diseases are usually caused by poor water quality, overcrowding, stress, injuries, poor nutrition, or introducing sick fish into a healthy pond without quarantine.

Ich (White Spot Disease) is caused by a tiny parasite. Signs: small white spots on the skin and fins, and fish rubbing against objects.

Fin Rot is caused by bacteria, often from dirty water. Signs: ragged, frayed, or discolored fins.

Dropsy is caused by a bacterial infection inside the body. Signs: a swollen, bloated belly and scales that stick outward.

Gill Disease is caused by parasites or bacteria on the gills. Signs: gasping at the surface and fast, heavy gill movement.

Fungal Infection often grows on wounds. Signs: cotton-like white or gray patches on the body.

Let's Learn Prevention & Control

Keep water clean and well-oxygenated by checking its quality often — most fish diseases start with poor water.

Avoid overcrowding — too many fish in a small space causes stress and spreads sickness quickly.

Quarantine new fish for a few days before adding them to the main pond, so any hidden disease doesn't spread.

Give fish proper, balanced feeding — well-fed fish have stronger natural defenses.

Clean and disinfect tools, nets, and containers regularly to avoid spreading germs between ponds.

Remove sick or dead fish right away so the illness doesn't spread to healthy fish.

If disease appears, farmers may use treatments like salt baths or approved medicines, and should ask a trained technician for serious cases.

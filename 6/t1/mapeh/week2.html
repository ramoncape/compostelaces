📖 What Is Holistic Wellness?

Holistic Wellness means taking care of every part of yourself — your body, mind, emotions, relationships, spirit, and more. The word "holistic" comes from the Greek word holos, meaning "whole."

Imagine a wheel with nine spokes. If one spoke is broken, the wheel wobbles and can't roll smoothly. That's how your wellness works — when one part is struggling, it affects all the others.

🧠 Think About It

Have you ever noticed that when you're stressed about school (mental wellness), you might also feel sick to your stomach (physical wellness)? Or when you fight with a friend (social wellness), you feel sad (emotional wellness)? This is holistic wellness in action!

💪 Physical Wellness

Physical wellness is about taking care of your body so it can function at its best. It includes exercise, sleep, nutrition, hygiene, and avoiding harmful substances.

✅ Habits that HELP
  • 60+ minutes of physical activity daily
  • 8–10 hours of sleep for teens
  • Eating a balanced, colorful diet
  • Regular hand-washing and hygiene
  • Drinking 6–8 glasses of water daily
❌ Habits that HARM
  • Too much screen time and sitting
  • Skipping meals or eating junk food
  • Irregular sleep schedules
  • Ignoring body pain or illness
  • Substance use or smoking

🏋️ Did You Know?

The Philippine Department of Health recommends at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity every day for children and youth. Even walking, dancing, or playing traditional games like patintero and tumbang preso count!

🧠 Mental / Intellectual Wellness

Mental/Intellectual wellness means keeping your mind active, curious, and healthy. It includes learning, critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and managing mental health.

Mental wellness ≠ Mental illness. Mental wellness is about how you think and process the world. You can be mentally well even while facing challenges if you have good coping strategies and seek help when needed.

❤️ Emotional Wellness

Emotional wellness is the ability to understand, manage, and express your feelings in healthy ways. It also means being able to handle life's challenges without being overwhelmed.

🎭 Emotion vs. Reaction

😤 Unhealthy Emotional Coping
  • Bottling up feelings until you explode
  • Hurting yourself or others when angry
  • Withdrawing completely from friends
  • Denying that you have any emotions
😊 Healthy Emotional Coping
  • Naming your emotions ("I feel anxious")
  • Talking to a trusted person
  • Physical activity to release tension
  • Journaling, drawing, or creative outlets

💬 Emotional Scenario Analysis

👥 Expert Group 1: Social Wellness

Social wellness refers to the quality of your relationships and your ability to connect meaningfully with others. It involves communication, empathy, respect, and building a support network.

Key Elements of Social Wellness

  • Healthy communication — Expressing yourself clearly and listening actively
  • Empathy — Understanding and sharing the feelings of others
  • Setting boundaries — Knowing your limits in relationships
  • Community involvement — Participating in barangay, school, and family life
  • Conflict resolution — Solving disagreements peacefully
Filipino Context: In Philippine culture, the concept of "kapwa" (shared identity with others) is central to social wellness. We are inherently connected. Family ("pamilya") and community ("komunidad") are deeply valued.

🙏 Expert Group 2: Spiritual Wellness

Spiritual wellness means having a sense of purpose, meaning, and values that guide your life. It does NOT have to be religious — it includes your personal beliefs, ethics, and what you feel is important.

What does spiritual wellness look like? ▼
  • Having a personal code of values (honesty, compassion, fairness)
  • Finding meaning in daily activities, not just big events
  • Practicing gratitude — noticing and appreciating what you have
  • Feeling a sense of connection to something greater than yourself (nature, community, God, or the universe)
  • Being able to ask deep questions about life, suffering, and purpose
How does spiritual wellness affect health? ▼

Research shows that people with a strong sense of purpose tend to:

  • Recover faster from illness and surgery
  • Live longer, healthier lives
  • Handle stress and grief better
  • Make healthier lifestyle choices
  • Feel more satisfied with their relationships

🌿 Expert Group 4: Environmental Wellness

Environmental wellness means living in harmony with your surroundings — creating safe, clean, and inspiring spaces — and caring for the natural world around you.

Two Levels of Environment

🏠 Personal Environment

Your home, bedroom, school, and barangay. A clean, organized, and safe personal environment reduces stress and supports focus and sleep.

🌱 Natural Environment

Air, water, forests, oceans, and biodiversity. As a Filipino, living near nature (mountains, beaches, forests) is a unique wellness resource!

🛠️ Occupational / Vocational Wellness

Occupational wellness means finding personal satisfaction and purpose in your work, studies, or activities. Even as a Grade 6 student, your "occupation" is being a student — and how you feel about it matters!

Signs of Poor Occupational Wellness
  • Dreading going to school every day
  • Feeling like your work has no purpose
  • No goals or ambitions for the future
  • Burnout — feeling exhausted and unmotivated
  • Poor work-life balance (all work, no play)
Signs of Strong Occupational Wellness
  • Excited to learn new things
  • Doing tasks that match your skills and interests
  • Having career goals and working toward them
  • Finding balance between school, hobbies, and rest
  • Feeling valued and respected for your efforts
For Filipino Students: Many young Filipinos carry the hope of their family. While this can be motivating, it can also create pressure. Occupational wellness means finding YOUR purpose — not just fulfilling expectations.

🔍 Case Analysis: The Story of Carlo

📋 Case Background

Carlo is a 13-year-old from a small barangay in Cebu. His father recently lost his job at a local factory that closed down. Carlo's family is now struggling financially. Carlo had to start selling snacks outside school to help earn money. He is tired, stressed, and skipping his homework. He has been avoiding his friends because he feels ashamed. He stopped attending their community's folk dance practices, which he used to love. He has been eating less because they can't always afford full meals.

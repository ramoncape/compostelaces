What Do You See? What Have You Tried?

Think about online meetings you've seen or joined. Answer these questions! 💬

What do you see in an online class or video meeting screen?
You see small video tiles of each person's face, a chat box on the side, buttons to mute/unmute the microphone, and a button to turn the camera on or off. There's also a "raise hand" button so you can signal when you want to speak without interrupting! 🖐️
Have you experienced being in an online class or family video call? What did you do?
In an online class, you listen to your teacher, answer questions in the chat, raise your hand virtually, and turn on your camera when asked. In family calls, you see and talk to relatives even when they are far away — that's the power of web conferencing! 🌏
Why do you think people prefer video calls over just phone calls?
Video calls let you see the other person's face, expressions, and body language — just like talking in person! You can share your screen, show objects, and feel more connected. For school, teachers can show presentations and check if students understand. 📱

Understanding Web Conferencing

Tap each card to expand and learn! 📚

💻 What is Web Conferencing? ▼
Web conferencing is a type of online communication that allows two or more people to hold a meeting, class, or discussion over the internet using audio, video, and text in real time.

The word "web" refers to the internet, and "conferencing" means holding a meeting or discussion. So web conferencing simply means holding a meeting through the internet!

You need: a device (computer, tablet, or smartphone), a microphone, a camera, and a stable internet connection.
📱 Common Web Conferencing Platforms ▼
🎥 Zoom — Free for 40-minute meetings; widely used in schools and businesses. Has breakout rooms, whiteboard, and reactions.

🟢 Google Meet — Made by Google; free and browser-based; integrates with Google Classroom and Gmail; preferred by many Philippine schools.

🟦 Microsoft Teams — Best for organizations; combines meetings, chat, and file management; used by companies and universities.

📘 Facebook Rooms / Messenger — Easy access for Filipino families; connects with existing Facebook accounts; up to 50 participants.
🎓 Uses in Education ▼
Web conferencing changed education forever — especially during the COVID-19 pandemic! Here's how it's used in schools:

• Online classes — Teachers teach remotely while students attend from home.
• Group discussions — Classmates collaborate through breakout rooms.
• Presentations — Students present reports by sharing their screen.
• Parent meetings — Schools hold Parent-Teacher conferences online.
• Webinars — Experts share knowledge with students and teachers via video.
📡 Uses in Communication ▼
Beyond school, web conferencing is used in many areas of life:

• Business meetings — Employees from different cities or countries meet without traveling.
• Medical consultations — Doctors see patients online (telemedicine/teleconsult).
• Family reunions — OFW families connect with loved ones abroad.
• Government — Officials hold meetings and briefings online.
• Religious services — Churches and mosques broadcast services online.

Spot the Difference!

Look at these two online meeting situations. What is wrong? What is right? 👀

❌ Scenario A — Improper Meeting

  • 🔊Microphone is ON but no one is speaking — background noise fills the call
  • 👕Student is in pajamas lying in bed
  • 🎮Playing a game on another window during the meeting
  • 💬Sending unrelated emoji in the chat
  • 🗣️Interrupting the teacher while speaking
  • 📸Taking screenshots of classmates without permission

✅ Scenario B — Proper Meeting

  • 🔇Microphone is MUTED when not speaking
  • 👔Student is dressed appropriately and seated properly
  • 👁️Focused on the screen with no distractions
  • 💬Uses chat only for lesson-related messages
  • 🖐️Uses "raise hand" feature before speaking
  • 🔒Does not share screen or photos without permission
👩‍🏫 Teacher Note: Have learners identify specific problems in Scenario A one by one. Then contrast with Scenario B. This creates concrete mental models for netiquette.
🔍
Explore
What's Wrong? What's Right?

Answer these questions based on the scenarios above! 💬

What is wrong in Scenario A (the improper meeting)?
The student is distracted (playing games), being disrespectful (pajamas, lying in bed), disturbing others (mic noise), being rude (interrupting), using chat incorrectly, and violating privacy (taking screenshots). These are all examples of poor netiquette and unsafe behavior.
What makes Scenario B the correct behavior?
In Scenario B, the student respects others by muting when not speaking, staying focused, using features properly, waiting their turn, and protecting everyone's privacy. This shows digital citizenship — being a responsible member of the online community! 🏅
Why should we follow rules in an online meeting?
Rules keep the meeting productive and respectful for everyone. Just like classroom rules help learning, online meeting rules help communication. They also protect your privacy and safety — online spaces can be risky if you're not careful! 🛡️

Explain
How to Use Web Conferencing Properly

Tap each card to learn! Practice the toggles too! 📚

🔗 How to Join a Web Conference ▼
Steps to join a web conference:

1. Get the meeting link or ID from your teacher or organizer.
2. Click the link or open the app and enter the Meeting ID and password.
3. Test your mic and camera before entering the meeting room.
4. Enter your real name — do not use nicknames that others won't recognize.
5. Wait in the waiting room until the host admits you.
6. Mute your mic immediately when you enter.
🎤 Using the Microphone ▼
Microphone Rules:

✅ MUTE when not speaking — Background noise like fans, TV, or pets can disrupt the meeting for everyone.
✅ UNMUTE only when it's your turn — Wait until you're asked to speak or use the raise hand feature.
✅ Speak clearly and at a normal pace — Don't shout or whisper; position the mic correctly.
❌ Never leave an open mic near a TV or noisy area.

🎤 My MicrophoneOFF
📷 Using the Camera ▼
Camera Rules:

✅ Turn ON when asked by the teacher — showing your face shows respect and helps the teacher see you're engaged.
✅ Choose a clean, appropriate background — sit in a well-lit, tidy area.
✅ Dress appropriately — treat the online class like a real classroom.
❌ Don't point the camera at personal items in your home — protect your family's privacy.
❌ Don't make faces or distracting gestures when on camera.

📷 My CameraOFF
💬 Using the Chat Box ▼
Chat Box Rules:

✅ Use it to ask questions related to the lesson.
✅ Answer when asked — type your response clearly and completely.
✅ Use proper language — write in Filipino or English as required; no slang or text speak in formal settings.
❌ Never spam or send irrelevant messages — this is disrespectful and distracting.
❌ Never share personal information in the chat — protect your privacy!
🛡️ Netiquette & Safety Rules ▼
Netiquette = Internet + Etiquette = proper online behavior.

🔒 Safety Rules:
• Never share the meeting link or ID publicly — only authorized people should join.
• Do not join meetings from strangers or unknown links.
• Tell a trusted adult if someone is behaving inappropriately in the meeting.
• Never record the meeting without everyone's permission.

🤝 Respect Rules:
• Use the "raise hand" before speaking.
• Do not interrupt classmates or the teacher.
• Be kind in chat — never bully or mock classmates.
👩‍🏫 Teacher Note: The toggle switches for Mic and Camera are interactive — use them to demonstrate muting concepts. Have the class try toggling on their own devices.

📌 What is Web Conferencing?

Web conferencing is the use of the internet to hold real-time meetings, classes, or presentations between people in different locations. It allows participants to see each other via video, hear each other through audio, share screens, chat in text, and collaborate on documents — all at the same time.

🗂️ Types of Web Conferencing

  • Video Conferencing – Two-way audio and video communication (e.g., Google Meet, Zoom). Best for classes, group discussions, and team meetings.
  • Webinars – One-to-many presentations where the audience mainly listens and asks questions (e.g., Webex Webinars, Zoom Webinar). Best for lectures, seminars, and training.
  • Web Meetings – Collaborative sessions with screen sharing and interactive tools. Best for project planning and group work.
  • Group Video Calls – Informal video calls for small groups (e.g., Messenger, Viber). Best for family check-ins and friend chats.

✅ Purposes of Web Conferencing

  • Education: Online classes, tutorials, group study sessions
  • Work: Remote meetings, client presentations, team coordination
  • Healthcare: Teleconsultation with doctors (telemedicine)
  • Government: Virtual briefings and public hearings
  • Social: Connecting with family and friends

💡 Samples of Effective Usage

  • A teacher hosts a Google Meet class where students share their screens to present projects.
  • A company holds a Zoom meeting for employees working from home to discuss quarterly reports.
  • A doctor consults with a patient via video call, reducing the need to travel.
  • A student attends a free online webinar to learn coding from an expert abroad.
🇵🇭

Philippine Context: The Department of Education (DepEd) used Google Meet and Zoom heavily during the pandemic. Many LGUs also held virtual barangay meetings to reach constituents safely.

🟢 Google Meet

Google Meet is a free video conferencing tool by Google. It is integrated with Google Workspace (Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Classroom) making it popular in Philippine schools.

  • Host Controls: Mute participants, remove attendees, lock the meeting
  • Screen Share: Share your entire screen, a window, or a browser tab
  • Captions: Real-time subtitles (helps students who have hearing difficulties)
  • Breakout Rooms: Divide students into smaller groups
  • Whiteboard: Collaborate on a virtual drawing board (via Jamboard)

🔵 Zoom

Zoom is one of the most popular global video conferencing tools, known for its stability and rich features even on slower internet connections.

  • Breakout Rooms: Easy to set up group activities
  • Polls: Quick in-meeting surveys
  • Virtual Backgrounds: Replace your background with any image
  • Reactions: Raise hand, thumbs up, clap without speaking
  • Recording: Record the meeting for students who missed it

🟣 Microsoft Teams

MS Teams combines video conferencing with a full workspace — chat channels, file sharing, assignments, and apps all in one place.

  • Channels: Organize discussions by subject or topic
  • Together Mode: Places all participants in a shared virtual space
  • Assignments: Teachers can post and grade tasks inside Teams
  • Integration: Works with all Microsoft 365 apps (Word, PowerPoint, Excel)

📋 Common Interface Parts & Functions

  • 🎤 Microphone Button – Mute or unmute yourself. Always mute when not speaking!
  • 📷 Camera Button – Turn your video on or off.
  • 🖥️ Share Screen – Show your screen to all participants.
  • 💬 Chat Panel – Type messages without interrupting the speaker.
  • 👥 Participants Panel – See who is in the meeting.
  • ✋ Raise Hand – Signal that you want to speak or ask a question.
  • 🔴 End/Leave – Exit the meeting. (Hosts can end for everyone.)

🌟 Practical Tips for Better Web Conferencing

  • Lighting: Sit facing a window or light source — not with your back to it.
  • Background: Use a clean, neutral background or a virtual background.
  • Internet: If possible, connect using a LAN cable instead of WiFi for stability.
  • Headset: Use earphones to reduce echo and background noise.
  • Mute when idle: Always mute your mic when you're not talking.
  • Test first: Test your camera and mic 10 minutes before the meeting.
  • Be on time: Join 2–3 minutes early to check your connection.
  • Minimize distractions: Close other browser tabs and apps to save bandwidth.

Safety rules, laws & responsible use

▼

✅ Safety Precautions in Web Conferencing

  • Use strong passwords for your accounts and meeting rooms.
  • Enable waiting rooms — don't let anyone in automatically.
  • Only join verified meetings — from your teacher or school only.
  • Lock the meeting after all participants have joined.
  • Use the "Remove" feature to kick out uninvited guests.
  • Do not share meeting links publicly on social media.
  • Log out of all accounts after every session.
  • Update your app regularly to get the latest security patches.

✅ DOs During Web Conferencing

  • DO dress appropriately — treat online meetings like face-to-face ones.
  • DO mute yourself when not speaking to minimize noise.
  • DO ask permission before recording any session.
  • DO use your real name as your display name in school meetings.
  • DO pay attention and stay focused on the meeting.
  • DO use the chat for questions rather than interrupting.
  • DO inform the host if there is a technical problem.

❌ DON'Ts During Web Conferencing

• DON'T eat loudly or have a messy background.

• DON'T share the meeting link with people who are not invited.

• DON'T record or screenshot other participants without their consent.

• DON'T use disrespectful or offensive language in chat or in speech.

• DON'T open or share inappropriate content during a meeting.

• DON'T let other people use your account or login credentials.

• DON'T reveal personal information (address, ID numbers) during sessions.

🔒 Online Privacy & Security Practices

  • Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Enable this on all accounts to add an extra layer of protection.
  • Don't reuse passwords: Use a unique, strong password for each account.
  • Beware of phishing: Fake meeting invites may steal your login info.
  • Blur or replace your background: Protect your home environment from being seen.
  • Private networks: Avoid joining meetings on public/unsecured WiFi.
  • Camera covers: Use a physical camera cover when not in a meeting.

🇵🇭 Relevant Philippine Laws

Republic Act 10175 – Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012

This law punishes cyber offenses including online harassment, cyberbullying, illegal access to computer systems, and cyber libel. Penalties range from prison time to fines. This applies fully to web conferencing — bullying a classmate in a Zoom meeting is a crime.

Republic Act 10173 – Data Privacy Act of 2012

This law protects the personal information of every Filipino. Recording someone in a web conference without consent, or sharing personal data collected during meetings, may violate this law. The National Privacy Commission (NPC) enforces it.

Republic Act 9995 – Anti-Photo and Video Voyeurism Act of 2009

It is illegal to take photos or videos of a person — especially in private situations — without consent and to distribute them. Taking screenshots of classmates in embarrassing situations during online class and sharing them online violates this law.

Republic Act 10627 – Anti-Bullying Act of 2013

This law requires all schools to adopt anti-bullying policies, including for cyberbullying. Online harassment of a student during a web conference by a schoolmate falls under this law, and schools are required to act on it.

Republic Act 11930 – Anti-OSAEC and Anti-CSAEM Act of 2022

This is the newest law that addresses Online Sexual Abuse and Exploitation of Children (OSAEC). It protects minors from being sexually exploited through online platforms. All web conferencing participants — especially minors — are protected by this law.

🧑‍⚖️

Remember: Ignorance of the law is not an excuse (ignorantia legis non excusat). Being a student does not exempt you from legal accountability. Your parents or guardians may also be held responsible for your actions online.

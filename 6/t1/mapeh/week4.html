📖 Learn — The Six Dimensions of Health

Tap each dimension to learn more

The World Health Organization (WHO) defines health as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being — not just the absence of disease. Scientists have identified six key dimensions of health.

💪

Physical

How well your body works. Includes exercise, sleep, nutrition, hygiene, and freedom from disease.

🧠

Mental / Intellectual

Your ability to think clearly, learn, solve problems, and use your mind in healthy ways.

❤️

Emotional

Understanding and expressing your feelings in healthy ways. Being able to cope with stress and sadness.

🤝

Social

Your ability to build positive relationships with family, friends, and your community.

✨

Spiritual

Having a sense of purpose, values, and meaning in life. It may or may not involve religion.

🌿

Environmental

Living in a safe, clean environment and taking care of the world around you.

💡 Key Idea: All six dimensions are connected. When one dimension is affected — for example, if you are physically sick — your emotional, social, and mental health can also be impacted.

🌱 Health & Adolescence

Why Does This Matter to You?

You are an adolescent — a person between 10 and 19 years old. This is one of the most important stages of human development. Your body, mind, and emotions are all changing rapidly.

🏃

Physical Changes

During adolescence, the body grows quickly. You may notice changes in height, weight, body shape, and the development of secondary sex characteristics. These changes are normal and natural.
🎭

Emotional Ups and Downs

Hormones affect your mood. You may feel excited, sad, angry, or confused — sometimes all in one day! Learning to understand and manage these emotions is part of healthy emotional development.
👫

Social Identity

Friendships become very important during adolescence. You begin to think about who you are, where you belong, and what values you hold. Peer pressure can also become a challenge during this stage.
🔍

Search for Identity

Adolescents often ask "Who am I?" This is healthy! You are developing your own values, beliefs, and sense of self. Spiritual and mental health dimensions are especially active during this time.

✅ Positive Changes That Improve Well-Being

Building Healthy Habits

Not all changes are negative! Positive changes in your life can strengthen multiple health dimensions at once. Tap each habit to see how it helps:

😴

Getting 8–10 hours of sleep

Physical: Body repairs itself. Mental: Better concentration and memory. Emotional: More stable mood and lower irritability. Sleep is one of the most powerful health habits!
🥦

Eating nutritious food

Physical: Stronger immune system and body. Mental: Brain foods (omega-3, leafy greens) improve focus. Emotional: Balanced blood sugar = better mood. Good food fuels all dimensions!
📖

Reading or learning new things

Mental: Develops critical thinking and knowledge. Emotional: Builds confidence and self-worth. Spiritual: Reading about values and life can deepen your sense of purpose.
🤲

Helping others / community service

Social: Builds strong, positive relationships. Spiritual: Gives a sense of meaning and purpose. Emotional: Helping others boosts happiness and self-esteem.

Case Study 1

🧒 Aisha's Social Media Struggle

Aisha, 12 years old, recently started spending 5–6 hours daily on social media. She began comparing herself to other people's photos online and felt ugly and inferior. She started skipping meals to "look better," and withdrew from family dinners. Her grades started dropping as she stayed up late scrolling her phone.

Case Study 2

🧒 Carlos Joins the School Team

Carlos, 11 years old, was shy and had very few friends. His teacher encouraged him to join the school basketball team. At first, he was nervous and made many mistakes. But over time, he practiced daily, improved his skills, and made new friends. His confidence grew, and he started participating more in class discussions.

⚖️ Compare — Positive vs. Negative Changes

Aisha vs. Carlos — What's the Difference?

Both Aisha and Carlos went through changes in Grade 6. But the outcomes were very different.

🔴 Aisha's change (spending too much time on social media) led to negative impacts across multiple dimensions — physical, mental, emotional, and social health all suffered.

🟢 Carlos's change (joining the basketball team) led to positive impacts — physical fitness improved, social connections grew, and his emotional and mental health strengthened.

📌 Analyzing cause-and-effect relationships across health dimensions.
🌊 The Ripple Effect

One change can affect everything

Scientists and health experts agree that the six dimensions of health do not work in isolation. A change in one dimension sends "ripples" to all others. Let's explore this using a real adolescent situation.

💡 Definition: The ripple effect in health means that a change or problem in one health dimension causes related changes in other dimensions — just like how a single stone dropped in a pond creates waves that spread outward.

Ripple Scenario

😰 Sam's Stressful Exam Week

Sam has a major exam next week. He is so worried that he cannot sleep at night. Because he is not sleeping well, he feels tired all day and does not eat properly. He becomes irritable and snaps at his friends, who then avoid him. He feels lonely and begins to doubt his abilities. By the end of the week, Sam feels completely overwhelmed.

🛡️ Coping Strategies for Adolescents

How to Handle Changes in Health Dimensions

Understanding what is happening to your health is the first step. The second step is knowing how to cope and respond in healthy ways.

🗣️

Talk to a Trusted Adult (Social + Emotional)

When you feel overwhelmed, talking to a parent, teacher, or school counselor can help you process your emotions and find solutions. Keeping problems inside can make them feel bigger than they are. Asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
🏃

Physical Activity (Physical + Emotional + Mental)

Exercise releases endorphins — natural chemicals in your brain that reduce stress and improve mood. Even 30 minutes of walking, dancing, or playing a sport can significantly improve your emotional and mental health.
📔

Journaling (Emotional + Mental)

Writing about your feelings and experiences helps you process emotions and think more clearly. It also helps you notice patterns — for example, noticing that you feel worst after too much screen time.
🌄

Time in Nature (Environmental + Physical + Spiritual)

Spending time outdoors — walking in a park, gardening, or just sitting under a tree — reduces cortisol (stress hormone) levels, boosts mood, and can create feelings of peace and connection to something bigger than yourself.
🙏

Prayer, Meditation, or Reflection (Spiritual + Emotional)

Having a spiritual practice — whether it's prayer, meditation, or simply quiet reflection — can provide a sense of calm, purpose, and perspective when life feels chaotic. Many adolescents find comfort in their faith or in moments of stillness.
⚠️ Barriers to Good Health

What stops adolescents from being healthy?

Even if adolescents know what's healthy, many face real barriers that make healthy choices difficult. Understanding these barriers is important for finding solutions.

📊 Examples of Health Barriers for Filipino Adolescents:
• Limited access to nutritious food (poverty)
• Peer pressure to engage in risky behavior
• Unsafe neighborhoods limiting outdoor activity
• Social media and internet addiction
• Mental health stigma (fear of being judged for having feelings)
• Lack of health education or information
